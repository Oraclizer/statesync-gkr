\title{Correctness of a Sparse-Merkle-Tree Verification-Circuit Compiler}
\author{Jinwook Kim}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A verification-circuit compiler turns sparse-Merkle-tree operations
(membership, non-membership, single-leaf update) into layered
arithmetic circuits whose acceptance is meant to coincide with the
native semantics of the operation: the circuit accepts a witness
encoding exactly when the operation holds between the claimed roots.
This entry mechanizes that claim and its composition with the GKR
protocol assembly of the parent session.

The development covers: the native semantics of sparse-Merkle-tree
operations (path verification against abstract leaf and node hashes);
a deep-embedded model of the compiler emitting one residual output
layer (equality, booleanity and tag residuals) over an evaluation
stack computing the in-circuit hash images and key-bit multiplexers;
Theorem A, the deterministic equivalence between circuit acceptance
and the native semantics, split into an acceptance-to-constraint stage
and a constraint-to-semantics stage, packaged as soundness and
completeness of a verifier model that mirrors the public-input checks
of the surrounding verifier; an injectivity lemma for the fixed-width
leaf pre-image encoding, reducing leaf-hash collisions to collisions
of the underlying sponge; and Theorem C, the composition bounding the
probability that the GKR chain accepts a semantically invalid
operation, for any adversary, by
$(r + \sum_i (2 s_i d + 1))/|\mathbb{F}|$.

No collision resistance or injectivity of the hashes is assumed
anywhere in Theorem A: the equivalence is deterministic, with the
hashes as abstract parameters.  The hash sub-circuits of the
evaluation stack are specified by a generator-correctness assumption
and remain otherwise abstract.

Every locale of the development is inhabited by a concrete instance, so
none of the theorems is vacuously true.  In particular the compiler
model is interpreted over the KoalaBear prime field
$\mathrm{GF}(2^{31} - 2^{24} + 1)$, the wire field of the production
prover, with an explicit gate list whose evaluation provably equals the
specification vectors; Theorem A is exercised there on a concrete
membership operation, and Theorem C fires with each of its premises
discharged for a concrete, semantically unsatisfiable update, the
representative degree bound being supplied by the polynomial
interpolation of the parent session.
\end{abstract}
